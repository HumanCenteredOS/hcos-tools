# Validation

HCOS™ recognizes that every prompt, tool, instrument, and assessment is an evolving human system.

Validation is not intended to prove that a document is perfect.

Its purpose is to continuously improve the quality, usefulness, safety, fairness, and real-world effectiveness of HCOS resources through thoughtful observation, evidence, and learning.

---

# Why Validation Matters

Every HCOS resource is designed to help people.

If a resource does not improve understanding, decision-making, communication, or human experience, it should continue to evolve.

Validation allows HCOS to learn from:

- real-world use
- user feedback
- expert review
- implementation experience
- new evidence
- changing standards
- unintended consequences

Rather than asking,

> "Is this finished?"

HCOS asks,

> "What can we learn to make this better?"

---

# Evidence-Based Improvement

HCOS encourages improvements that are supported by observable evidence whenever practical.

Evidence may include:

- User feedback
- Hiring manager feedback
- Recruiter feedback
- ATS compatibility testing
- Subject matter expert review
- Peer review
- Case studies
- Workflow observations
- Usability testing
- Published literature
- Professional guidelines
- Lessons learned during implementation

Not every improvement requires formal research.

However, meaningful revisions should be informed by something more than personal preference whenever possible.

---

# Continuous Learning

Validation is an ongoing process rather than a final milestone.

As new information becomes available, HCOS resources should continue to improve.

Examples include:

- clarifying confusing language
- correcting inaccuracies
- improving accessibility
- simplifying instructions
- strengthening examples
- expanding guidance
- improving safety
- reducing unintended bias
- increasing consistency across HCOS documents

Every revision is an opportunity to improve future versions.

---

# Human-Centered Validation

HCOS evaluates more than technical accuracy.

Validation should also consider whether a resource:

- preserves human dignity
- respects individual differences
- communicates clearly
- supports informed decision-making
- avoids unnecessary harm
- reduces confusion
- improves collaboration
- strengthens systems rather than creating unnecessary complexity

A technically correct resource may still require revision if it creates misunderstanding or unnecessary burden.

---

# Validation Sources

When practical, document the source of important improvements.

Examples include:

| Source | Example |
|---------|---------|
| User Feedback | Resume users found a section difficult to understand. |
| ATS Testing | Keywords were not consistently recognized by common ATS software. |
| Recruiter Review | Recruiters recommended reorganizing the experience section. |
| Hiring Manager Review | Managers requested clearer demonstration of measurable contributions. |
| Subject Matter Expert | Clinical terminology was updated for greater accuracy. |
| Literature Review | Guidance aligned with newly published evidence or professional standards. |

Documenting the source of revisions improves transparency and helps future contributors understand why changes were made.

---

# Validation Documentation

When significant improvements occur, consider documenting:

- what changed
- why it changed
- supporting evidence
- limitations
- remaining questions
- future validation needs

This information may be included within:

- CHANGELOG.md
- Release Notes
- Validation reports
- Supporting documentation

---

# Validation Does Not Mean Perfection

No framework is ever complete.

Healthcare evolves.

Technology evolves.

Organizations evolve.

People evolve.

HCOS is intended to evolve alongside them.

Validation is therefore an ongoing commitment to learning rather than a declaration of certainty.

---

# HCOS Validation Philosophy

The purpose of validation is not to defend previous work.

The purpose of validation is to improve future work.

Every observation is an opportunity to learn.

Every revision is an opportunity to better serve people.

Every improvement strengthens the HCOS framework for those who use it next.
